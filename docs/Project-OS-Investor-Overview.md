# Project OS

## The visual operating system for building software

**Project OS turns software into a living workspace that people can understand, direct, and evolve with AI.**

We are building an environment where a founder can point to a product experience, explain what should change, and follow that intention through the system that implements it. Architecture, interface, code, agent activity, and verification become connected parts of the same workspace.

Our ambition is to make creating and operating software accessible to everyone with the judgment to build something valuable. We want people to spend more of their effort deciding what should exist, with the understanding and control to bring it into the world.

## The opportunity: give people control over software they can imagine

Our thesis is that as AI becomes better at producing code, the limiting factor moves toward human understanding and coordination. Builders still need to know where a change belongs, what it might affect, what the agent actually did, and whether the result works.

A product is a connected system: an interface depends on application logic, data, permissions, and other services. A request as simple as “change how checkout works” can cross all of them. When that system is difficult to see, delegating its implementation becomes difficult to supervise.

Project OS makes the system visible and actionable. It gives people a shared model of the product and connects their decisions to the work required to change it.

The opportunity extends across founders building their first product, designers shaping behavior, engineers navigating unfamiliar systems, and teams coordinating AI execution. Our long-term market is the work of creating and continuously improving software.

## The product: see it, direct it, follow it through

Project OS brings three connected experiences into one development environment.

### Understand the product as a system

A Function Map organizes the project into understandable responsibilities and relationships. Builders can move from the overall product into its systems, components, and supporting files. The map gives an AI conversation a place in the product: a feature, a dependency, a selected file, or a specific task.

The goal is an architecture that remains useful while work happens. A builder should be able to understand both what a part of the product does and how changing it connects to the rest of the system.

### Express intent where it belongs

Spatial captures let builders attach requests to what they can see. Arrows, annotations, and UI stencils provide concrete references for a desired change. A visual instruction can carry a location, a proposed component, an interaction, and the relevant project context into an agent request.

Consider a founder improving onboarding. They can identify the confusing step, annotate the screen, and describe the behavior they want. The product direction stays attached to its visual reference as the work moves toward implementation.

We are building toward a workflow in which visual direction, architecture, and implementation stay connected throughout the life of a change.

### Observe and govern execution

Agent activity is projected onto the project graph so builders can follow which resources are being read, changed, created, or verified. Context assembly and persisted run records provide a foundation for understanding what informed an execution and what happened during it.

Code review, permissions, verification results, and recoverable writes give the builder practical ways to supervise the work. As agent capability grows, our ambition is to make larger assignments understandable enough for people to delegate with confidence.

The product loop we are pursuing is straightforward: **understand the system, express a change, supervise execution, inspect the evidence, and carry the result forward.**

## What exists today

Project OS has an actively developed desktop workbench. The current implementation includes:

- A Function Map with C4 architecture views, structure scanning, and navigation into project files.
- Context-aware conversations, planning and debugging modes, code editing, diff review, and a terminal.
- Spatial capture sessions, annotations, pins, and UI stencils for expressing visual intent.
- Live graph overlays connecting recorded agent activity to project resources.
- An agent harness with instructions, rules, skills, permissions, execution records, and context assembly.
- Local project persistence, recoverable change transactions, and verification workflows.

These capabilities establish the foundation for the broader product. We are at an early development stage; broader repository support, workflow reliability, onboarding, and commercial validation remain priorities. The platform vision and commercial strategy below describe where we intend to take that foundation.

## Where we start, and how we expand

Our proposed entry market is small teams building web products with AI: founders, product-minded developers, and designers working closely with engineering. They have short feedback loops and a direct need to move from a visible product problem to an understandable implementation.

The initial promise is concrete: help a builder make a meaningful change in an existing product while understanding its scope and reviewing the outcome.

From that entry point, we intend to expand along the customer's own growth:

1. **Individual builders:** understand a project, express changes, and supervise an agent.
2. **Product teams:** share project context, review work, and coordinate execution around the same system.
3. **Organizations:** manage agent activity across projects with the policies, integrations, and operational controls that larger deployments require.

The distribution hypothesis is to earn adoption through useful work on a real repository. A successful change creates an opportunity for repeated use; shared maps and reviewable outcomes create an opportunity to bring collaborators into the workspace.

## A business that grows with responsibility

Our proposed business model combines subscriptions for the workspace with pricing that reflects agent execution and advanced capabilities.

Individual plans would serve builders using Project OS to understand and change their own products. Team plans would add shared context and coordination. Enterprise offerings would address organizational deployment, governance, and integration needs. Compute-intensive execution could be metered or connected to a customer's own model account, subject to product validation and provider support.

The commercial objective is to become a recurring part of how customers improve software. Pricing and packaging will be tested against demonstrated customer value and the economics of serving that work.

## The advantage we intend to build

The strategic asset is the connection between **product intent, system structure, execution, and outcome**.

A visual request becomes more useful when it knows its place in the architecture. An agent becomes easier to supervise when its activity is attached to the resources it changes. A project becomes easier to continue when relevant decisions and execution records survive beyond one conversation.

We believe those connections can create increasing product value as a workspace develops. Defensibility would have to be earned through the quality of that accumulated context, the reliability of the execution experience, and the depth of integration into daily work. Customer code remains the customer's asset; we do not assume ownership of it or a right to train on it.

Our model-provider abstraction supports a strategy in which Project OS can improve alongside advances in underlying models. We intend to own the experience of directing and understanding the work, while choosing models suited to the task.

## What we need to prove next

The next stage is about demonstrating repeatable value in real projects:

- Builders can understand an unfamiliar project well enough to make a consequential change.
- Visual instructions and project context reduce ambiguity during implementation.
- Execution records and verification help people identify problems and accept correct results.
- Customers return for ongoing work and are willing to pay for that capability.

These are the foundations for expanding from a useful individual tool into a shared environment for software creation.

## The company we are building

Our ambition is to establish Project OS as a primary workspace for human and AI software creation: a place where an idea can become an understandable system, where people can direct its evolution, and where the evidence of that work remains connected to the product.

The larger impact is participation. People with deep knowledge of a problem should have a practical path to building the software that solves it. Engineers should be able to apply their judgment across more ambitious systems. Small teams should be able to coordinate work that would otherwise exceed their capacity.

**Project OS is building toward a future in which more people can create consequential software—and retain the understanding to shape what it becomes.**
